What EHC is · now, next and at scale

Independent makers are too small to compete alone.EHC makes them big together.

Today’s reality for independent hot sauce makers

Making great sauce is the easy part.Selling it can be soul-destroying.

One maker becomes the warehouse, packing bench, export desk, sales team and advertising agency. On an average €8.84 bottle, domestic walk-in postage takes 41–47% of the customer’s payment. Before the sauce crosses a border, the courier can earn nearly as much as the person who made it. Across borders, even inside the EU, the economics collapse: shipping can cost more than the sauce.

At that point, customers do not reject the sauce. They abandon the basket.

When the maker stops, the business stops. Take a holiday; close the shop. Pitch a wholesaler or retailer; become one more tiny supplier with another invoice, another delivery and another problem for the buyer to solve. It is easier for retail to ignore the category than to stock it.

Online, the same maker enters an attention market dominated by mass brands and Amazon. The contest is unequal before the first ad runs. Their nights disappear into filming Instagram Reels and hoping that is enough to be noticed.

EHSA builds trust. ChiliFest.eu creates discovery and direct sales. Republic of Heat keeps that discovery alive year-round. ChiliFest.eu and Republic of Heat are growing. Following EHSA media coverage, one maker increased sales from a typical 50 bottles per month to 400 in five days and secured a retail contract.

The demand is there. The infrastructure to serve it is not.

What the makers still lack is shared power.That is the job of EHC: turn hundreds of small makers into one serious market force.

The problem, measured

Every order pays the courier first

Independent makers pay the highest published rates. The farther each bottle travels alone, the more of its value goes to the courier.

The anchor case · Ferment Island, Gozo

To get one of Europe’s best sauces, you’ll spend €20 on shipping. And €9 on the sauce.

69% of the order goes to the courier.

The same imbalance exists closer to home. On an €8.84 average bottle in the Republic of Heat catalogue, German walk-in postage of €6.19–7.69 consumes 41–47% of a one-bottle order.

The 42-producer checkout test

Only 9 of 42 makers showed a delivery price upfront.

  • The real price arrived lateMany customers had to build a basket before learning what delivery would cost.

The market & the category play

The market is growing. Independents are not.

The category play

Cheaper shipping is not the goal. A category with a name, a definition and a champion is.

Craft beer reached 24.7% of US beer dollars after building all three over four decades. That is a precedent, not a timetable. European hot sauce has not yet had the same organising moment.

What belongs

Craft Hot Sauce covers the chilli-led products made by the same independent producers: hot sauces, chilli crisps, chilli oils and sambals. It does not expand into the vague territory of “craft condiments.”

What makes it usable

The Maker Mark will define who qualifies. Independent heat testing will give buyers a consistent measure of heat. Together they give the category a boundary and a common language.

The 25-retailer category audit · Germany, Netherlands and wholesale

No mainstream grocer surveyed gives hot sauce a shelf of its own.

  • Zero craft shelvesNo retailer surveyed presented “Craft Hot Sauce” as a category.
  • Premium demand exists elsewhereSpecialists including Heatsupply, Pepperworld and Mexican Tears already sell the premium model.
  • Range is limited by inventoryEvery incumbent buys and holds stock, restricting the range to what its own warehouse can finance.

The unclaimed position

Europe has no curated, heat-specific consolidator.

EHC can occupy that position without buying the inventory: suppliers retain ownership and pay for the space they use. Existing stock-owning retailers cannot copy that model without changing their business.

Evidence is strongest for Germany, the Netherlands and Austria. Southern Europe is not yet covered, and category-growth evidence remains limited.

The business case

No stock to buy. No shelf space to subsidise.

Europe’s independent range can sit under one roof without EHC financing the stock or the space it occupies. Makers own the sauce and pay only for the shelf space and services they use.

Membership

Pays for the collective

A recurring annual fee funds access to shared buying power and routes to market.

Storage

Pays for every carton

Makers pay only for the shelf space they use, while their stock remains available across Europe.

Fulfilment

Pays when stock moves

EHC earns per parcel; carriage is passed through at the pooled contract rate.

The result

Every new member expands the range and the revenue, not the inventory bill.

Makers gain the reach and economics of a larger company. EHC grows with their activity, without gambling on which sauces will sell.
