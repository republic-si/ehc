What it costs you to sell your own sauce

The courier earns more from your bottle than you do.

EHC gets you a big company’s shipping, a range a buyer will actually take, and you keep your name on the bottle.

You are the warehouse, the packing bench, the export desk, the sales team and the advertising agency. On an average €8.84 bottle, domestic walk-in postage takes 41–47% of what your customer pays. Across a border, even inside the EU, it costs €10.49–11.99 to send that same bottle — more than the sauce inside it. Your customer does not reject your sauce. They abandon the basket.

And when you stop, the business stops. Take a holiday and you close the shop.

So how, exactly, are you supposed to grow?

You make the sauce. Then you buy the boxes, book the courier, eat the postage, learn the customs codes, register for packaging in six separate countries, chase the invoice, answer the emails, work the stall, shoot the photos, and start again on Monday. On an €8.84 bottle.

It is ridiculous. It is why hot sauce in Europe stays a hobby for most of us.

EHC exists to stop that. Not to make you a better hobbyist — to make this fucking real.

What you get, what you pay · 2 of 3

Already running, before you sign anything

Three things that already sell sauce for makers today.

EHSA

The European Hot Sauce Awards. Independent judging, and a medal a customer trusts and a buyer can defend.

ChiliFest.eu

Events where people taste your sauce, meet you and buy from you on the day. Three days at capacity, the queue roughly 700 metres.

Republic of Heat

One curated shop that keeps those customers buying all year. It already ships a three-bottle set for about what most makers charge to send one.

One maker’s normal month was 50 bottles. After an EHSA coverage wave they sold 400 in five days, and signed a retail contract off the back of it. One result, not a typical one. It is what recognition plus a route to market did for a maker in your position.

Four things you cannot do at your size. Get a customer’s first taste and keep them buying all year. Reach a trade buyer as one range instead of one small supplier. Carry a medal and a mark that buyer can defend upwards. Ship on pooled rates you could never reach on your own volume.

You own every bottle until we have a customer for it.

Your sauce sits in the hub in your name. We buy it from you only when we have somewhere for it to go — a Republic of Heat customer, or a trade buyer we have signed. We never buy stock we have not already sold, so nobody discounts it, dumps it, or decides which of your sauces deserves the space. Finding those buyers is the job you pay us to do. The risk stays where it is: you paid to make it, and you pay storage while it sits.

We do not replace you in your home market. Your own shop, your stall and your local stockists stay exactly as they are — we do not touch them and we do not want them. EHC's job is the order you were never going to get anyway: the customer two countries away who would have abandoned the basket at €10.49 postage, and the buyer who was never going to find you alone. Every order through the hub is an extra one, on top of what you already do.

What you pay

Membership

€150 per year
One flat fee for the founding cohort. Buys shared buying power, the Maker Mark and the routes to market.

Storage

€2–3 per carton, per month
A carton is a standard 400×300 Euro container: 48 bottles. You pay for the shelf space you use, and orders go out while you are asleep, at a wedding, or making the next batch.

Fulfilment

€3.50–3.70 per parcel
Charged when stock moves, and priced to cover picking, packing and materials — nothing else. Carriage is passed through at the pooled contract rate, with no mark-up.

You decide how deep to stock, and you can do the maths yourself. Storage is billed by the carton, by the month — 48 bottles, €2–3 — so there is no unit to fill and no lease to commit to. A typical member holds around 350 bottles: eight cartons, about €22 a month for their whole range. Stock deep and send one consignment a year. Stock shallow and send smaller ones more often. Either way you pay for the shelf you actually use.

Those are the only three charges, and there is no commission on your sales. Every maker who joins after you makes your share of the fixed costs smaller, which is the honest reason to be early rather than eightieth.

New, and it lands on 12 August 2026

Every country you sell into now wants its own packaging paperwork.

No exemption

The EU Packaging Regulation (2025/40) applies from 12 August 2026. Micro-producers are excused some of it, but not the producer responsibility fees.

Country by country

You register, report and pay in every member state your bottles reach. Where you are not established, you must appoint an authorised representative there.

And your box

Packaging has to be minimised, and an e-commerce box may be no more than 40% empty space. Penalties run to €200,000.

The fees themselves are small. German registration is free and a small maker’s licensing can be under €100 a year. The cost is the multiplication: one maker, six countries, six registers, six reports, six sets of rates. Doing that once, for everyone, is exactly what a trade body is for.

The next 90 days, and what they involve you in

Two things you can act on now. The warehouse comes after.

30 days

Enter EHSA 2027

The revamped awards launch with visitBerlin. Entries open to independent makers across Europe, with the format, categories, timetable and judging model published up front. You need no warehouse and no freight to enter.

90 days

Take one of the first 80 memberships

The Craft Hot Sauce Maker Mark goes live, with published criteria, governance and permitted claims. You get the written definition of an independent maker first, so you can check whether you qualify before you pay.

Six months: the hub opens and your stock goes in

It opens once enough founding makers have signed to cover its running costs. Until it does, none of your stock is in it, and we will not pretend otherwise.

Why this is worth more than a cheaper postage account: no mainstream grocer in our 25-retailer audit gives hot sauce a shelf of its own. Every incumbent has to buy your stock before it can sell it, which is why most of them never call you. A category with a name, a definition and a champion behind it is what puts your bottle on that shelf. Craft beer took four decades and reached 24.7% of US beer dollars. That is a precedent, not a timetable.
