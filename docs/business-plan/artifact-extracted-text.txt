What we do · one page
 We store hot sauce.We ship it.We make it easier to sell.
EHC — the European Heat Council — is a shared warehouse and shipping operation for Europe's craft hot sauce makers. They send us their stock. We store it, pick it, pack it, and ship it. When a shop or a wholesaler wants to buy, they order many makers at once, on one invoice, from us. That is the whole idea.
For makers
A small producer can sell across Europe without building their own warehouse, courier deals, or sales team. They keep making sauce. We handle storage, packing, and shipping.
For buyers
A shop orders 30 makers in one delivery, on one invoice, instead of chasing 30 producers one by one.
For shoppers
Sauces that used to never leave their home region turn up on shelves in other countries.
 The part that makes it safe
 We never own the stock. Producers own their bottles and pay us to store them. We buy a bottle only at the exact moment a sale fires, and resell it in the same instant. So we carry the whole category without ever tying up cash in inventory, or taking the risk of stock that does not sell.

H2: Every line earns its place, or it's out
In brief
Five rules decide whether a revenue line ships. They keep the model aligned with the makers it depends on. The spine holds throughout: EHC carries no inventory risk, and only wins when the maker wins.
Opt-in, not imposed.

Transparent take. The maker always sees the cut.
No lock-in, no exclusivity. No forever-tax, no hostage stock.

The takeaway

===== PANEL: p-lanes =====
02How a bottle moves
H2: Three lanes, three money mechanics
In brief
A producer's stock sits at the hub, producer-owned, storage paid. From there a bottle leaves one of three ways, and the money works differently in each. Keeping the lanes separate is the whole model.
Lane 1EHC resells to an external storeEHC is the seller
EHC buys at rate 1 → sells at rate 1 + volume margin (€3.00 → €0.40 / btl)
Buy and sell. Delivered by EHC's own van on the Berlin milk-run.
Lane 2EHC resells to ROHEHC is the seller
EHC buys at rate 1 → sells to ROH at rate 1 + €0.40 flat
Buy and sell. ROH retails the stock in its boxes and online.
Lane 3Producer's own sale — EHC fulfils onlyNo buy · no margin
Producer keeps the full sale → pays EHC a fulfilment fee
Play A, own box: €4.75/parcel plus box storage, no inserts.   Play B, ROH box: €3.70/parcel, and EHC earns inserts (~€0.75).
Rate 1 is the producer-to-EHC wholesale price, typically 30–50% off SRP depending on the maker.
Lanes 1 and 2 do not cannibalise Lane 3. They are EHC-generated demand, a store order or an ROH box, that the producer could not win alone. A producer who grows their own webshop and lives on Lane 3 is welcome; EHC still earns fulfilment, and it cost nothing to enable.
The margin stack is tight and volume-negotiated, Metro-style. Buy one and you pay near list. Buy five to ten and it gets serious. Buy a hundred and it lands near cost. The €3 is a small-order ceiling, not a universal margin: on a €9 SRP bought at 45% off there is only about €0.45 of room, so real orders slide down the curve as they grow.
The takeaway
Lanes 1–2 are demand EHC creates; Lane 3 is the maker's own sale that EHC simply ships. None cannibalises the others, and every lane earns the hub something.

===== PANEL: p-flags =====
Open flags & decisions
H2: What is settled, and what still needs a signature
In brief
The structure is decided: principal buy/sell via a UG that converts to GmbH, insured, with producer indemnities. Two operational questions stay open — real capacity, and dwell-time storage pricing.
Decided#1Seller-of-record liability
 The moment EHC buys in Lanes 1–2 it is the legal food seller, which brings product liability and requires a proper trading entity. Rather than dodge to disclosed-agent, which fragments the one-invoice moat, the plan stays principal (buy and sell) and caps the liability:
EHC starts as a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) and converts to GmbH later. Limited liability from day one on about €1 capital. A UG must retain 25% of annual profit to reserves until it reaches €25k capital, so roughly a quarter of early surplus stays in the company as forced saving toward the GmbH.
Product-liability insurance (Produkthaftpflicht). The UG shields the owners, but the company still carries seller liability, so insure it. Cheap for sealed, shelf-stable goods.
Producer indemnity clauses. Each maker warrants compliance and indemnifies EHC. EU product-liability law already channels manufacturing defects to the named producer.
Disclosed-agent stays as a fallback only for producers who cannot or will not be clean sellers.
Pending: a Steuerberater and lawyer to finalise the UG setup, the Kommissionär/VAT treatment, and the indemnity wording.
Open#2Capacity is not the same as cost
The founders' part-time pay is covered. The open question is whether the on-the-ground team can physically run 80 makers across three lanes, plus a van run, plus EHSA and AHSA, plus catalogues. The money works. The hours may not.
Open#3Storage is dwell-time driven
Fast movers pay little, slow movers pay more. The flat €0.40/btl/yr is a proxy for a real per-bottle-per-month rate.
MinorBottle size and the top wholesale tier
The model runs on 100 ml bottles, where real sauce is often 150–200 ml. The €3 top wholesale tier only survives on low-volume, high-shelf-price buyers.
